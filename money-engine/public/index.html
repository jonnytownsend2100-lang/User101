    Method, and what this cannot tell you

    The model

    Each month: revenue is units times price. Gross profit subtracts cost of delivery. Operating profit subtracts fixed costs and the cost of every customer you had to acquire that month — including replacements for the ones who churned. Tax applies only to positive operating profit; losses are never credited back.

    contribution per unit
  = price × (1 − delivery%)
    − acquisition cost × (growth% + churn%)

break-even units = fixed costs ÷ contribution per unit

    The churn term is the part most models get wrong. To grow 10% while losing 6%, you must acquire 16% of your base every month — you replace the leavers before you add anyone.

    Honest limits

    • It is linear and monthly. Real businesses are lumpy, seasonal, and pay late.
    • Cash timing is ignored: revenue is booked when earned, not when collected.
    • Growth and churn are held constant. Real curves bend — usually downward.
    • It cannot see demand. A perfect model of a business nobody wants is still worth zero.
    • The playbook ranges summarise other people's outcomes, not promises about yours.

    Use it to reject bad arithmetic early — that is where it earns its keep.
